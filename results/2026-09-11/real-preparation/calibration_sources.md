# Candidate likelihood provenance and minimal estimation protocol

Inspected 2026-09-11. Metadata/read-only source research; no weights downloaded and no model fitted by this task. Recommendations below are **new experimental model designs**, not pretrained VINDEX calibration claims.

## Primary semantics: an explicit encoding likelihood

A primary-source precedent is Naselaris et al., [Bayesian reconstruction of natural images from human brain activity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553889/), Neuron 2009, DOI `10.1016/j.neuron.2009.09.006`. Their decoder combines encoding distributions `p(response | image)` with image priors, fits encoding models on estimation stimuli, and reconstructs novel stimuli. This supports the direction of the likelihood, not our particular CLIP/PCA/ridge specification.

Recommended minimal new model: **subject-specific CLIP-feature ridge encoding with cross-fitted Gaussian residuals**. This leaves VINDEX/LLaVA as the brain-language backend, while the likelihood adapter honestly identifies its own fitted encoding model. For trial brain vector `b`, fixed image features `x(I)`, and subject `s`:

```
y = P_s ((b - m_s) / d_s)                 # optional low-dimensional brain projection
mu_s(I) = A_s x(I) + c_s
p_theta(y | I,s) = Normal(y; mu_s(I), Sigma_s)
ell_i = -0.5 * [(y-mu_s(I_i))' Sigma_s^-1 (y-mu_s(I_i))
                + logdet(Sigma_s) + d*log(2*pi)]
w_i = softmax(log(r_i) + ell_i)
```

`m_s,d_s,P_s,A_s,c_s,Sigma_s` are fitted only on permitted development training images. Use fixed pooled CLIP features to keep ridge small; optionally PCA to 64 brain dimensions, chosen before seeing test scores. This is a valid likelihood for observed brain statistic `y`, and should be described as `p(I | y(b),s)`, an approximation to `p(I|b,s)`. Do not claim a density over full voxels when a noninvertible projection is used. Full-voxel diagonal Gaussian is also computationally feasible but tends to overcount correlated evidence; reduced dimension plus shrinkage covariance is preferable.

Fit ridge on image-level weighted trials (each image total weight one). Estimate residual covariance from inner out-of-fold predictions, not training residuals: `Sigma=(1-rho)Cov(e)+rho*diag(Cov(e))+epsilon*I`. Predetermine a small ridge/shrinkage grid and select using development candidate log loss plus encoding prediction checks. If a scalar covariance multiplier `tau` is calibrated, use `Normal(mu,tau*Sigma)` with its normalization; do not present an arbitrary softened score as the original fitted Gaussian. Inner folds must estimate their own preprocessing/projections. Single-trial and repeat-averaged likelihoods differ: calibrate on the same repeat regime as evaluation, or explicitly model mean covariance `Sigma/R` only if the independence assumption is justified.

Alternative retaining BrainXS as the statistic: define `y=P f_VINDEX(b)` and regress `mu(I)` to it, then fit the same residual likelihood. This is a legitimate explicit model of the decoder statistic. However, if the released brain encoder saw the outer-held-out images during its original training, a new calibration split alone does not make end-to-end representations cross-fitted. Audit and disclose its original training image IDs; use disjoint held-out images or retrain representation folds where strict end-to-end cross-fitting is required. Direct raw-brain encoding avoids that particular supervised checkpoint leakage.

## Cross-fitting and candidate selection

1. Lock hidden test IDs before any fitting. Group by global NSD `image_id`, including all repetitions, sessions, captions, questions and subjects associated with the image. Keep a common group assignment across subjects. Save hashes of split manifests. Never select hyperparameters, stop training or filter cases from hidden-test VQA scores.
2. For each outer development fold `k`, fit the complete encoding/calibration pipeline on other image groups. Inner image-group folds handle ridge, dimension, covariance and any temperature selection. Produce candidate likelihoods for fold `k` only using its fitted artifact. Student training consumes these out-of-fold targets. Fit the final test adapter on development groups after choices are frozen.
3. Candidate bank definition must precede each evaluated brain trial and be independent of its answer, target identity, and ground-truth image features. A deterministic bank of permitted training images is the simplest retrieval setting. An externally fixed gallery containing novel test images is a distinct closed-gallery setting and must be declared as such; its images may be feature-encoded but its brain responses/answers must not enter fitting. Do not force-insert the true image into an ordinary retrieval bank.
4. Keep the same candidate IDs, masks and `r` for all correction variants on a trial. Prefer a fixed image-only gallery partition/subsample independent of `b` if a small K is essential. Brain-dependent top-K selection changes the support: describe posterior weights as conditional on that selected set, record full-bank recall/mass when available, and do not interpret the selected reference mixture as an unconditional no-brain prior. For no-evidence comparisons rerun the whole declared selector or use a shared brain-independent bank; reusing a correct-brain top-K bank leaks neural information into the control.
5. For an outer fold, fit learned selectors and bank statistics only outside the fold; fit any candidate-size or selection policy on inner folds. If target images are absent from a retrieval bank, report coverage separately and calibrate identification log loss on a dedicated closed-gallery calibration task whose selection rule is declared. Do not silently discard misses or add targets to compute an artificially favorable loss.
6. Save model coefficients/covariance, fold IDs, feature/checkpoint revisions, candidate-bank hash, prior and selector policy, subject/voxel mapping, and calibration sample IDs. Report held-out candidate NLL/Brier/reliability, entropy/IG, recall, per-session performance, and sensitivity to covariance scale.

## Zero evidence and negative controls

The exact contract is algebraic: `ell_i=constant` on valid candidates implies `w=r`, arithmetic correction zero, and `Q=S_anchor`. Implement a **no-evidence mask** returning `ell_i=0` for every valid candidate. This can accompany the zero-brain intervention, but must be declared explicitly.

A numerical zero voxel vector is an observed value, not missing data. A Gaussian encoding model generally assigns different likelihoods at zero because candidate means have unequal Mahalanobis norms. Biased nonlinear BrainXS also need not return zero features at zero input. Therefore do not assert that an unmasked zero vector automatically implies `w=r`. Retain a raw-zero diagnostic if relevant and report it separately from the no-evidence identity control. Likewise shuffled/noise/wrong-subject brains need not produce uniform posteriors; the required claim is that correct brain robustly beats those controls, not that they algebraically cancel.

A normalized-mean isotropic Gaussian on a zero-preserving statistic can make zero yield equal scores, but this is a different explicit model and requires separate validation; do not distort the primary encoder merely to force the identity. If CE remains enabled in the no-evidence training arm, its CE gradient remains; only the NPP contribution is zero. Optimizer weight decay/momentum can also update weights despite zero NPP gradient, so distinguish zero loss contribution from an unchanged parameter tensor.

## Compatibility ablation only

`softmax(log r + cosine/T)` with validation-fitted `T` can be a useful named calibrated-compatibility ablation. Temperature fitting alone does not verify a generative brain likelihood. A normalized von Mises-Fisher model with fixed concentration and unit directional features can justify a cosine-form log density under explicit assumptions, but its statistic, density normalization, concentration fitting, and held-out calibration must be documented as a newly estimated model. Do not relabel the current raw score to satisfy `score_semantics=log_likelihood` without this work.
